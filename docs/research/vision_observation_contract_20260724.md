# Vision Observation Contract Decision

Date: 2026-07-24

## Decision

Use a current grayscale image as the required visual signal. Keep temporal
representations configurable additions, not replacements:

```text
required:
  current grayscale frame

optional:
  prior appearance frames
  signed current-minus-previous grayscale delta
  thresholded absolute-change mask
```

The initial FlightRL default is `64x48`, one frame, normalized to `[-1, 1]`.
This is an iteration baseline, not a claim that `64x48` is optimal. Run
controlled resolution and temporal-channel ablations after the synchronized
camera/telemetry dataset exists.

## Evidence

The closest published hardware reference is PULP-Dronet v3. Its public dataset
uses the Crazyflie 2.1 and AI Deck Himax camera, records `324x244` grayscale
frames together with pilot yaw rate, state, and forward ToF data, and crops
training inputs to `200x200`. Its visual policy uses appearance frames rather
than replacing them with frame differences.

- Dataset:
  https://zenodo.org/records/13348430
- Paper:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12675

Tiny-PULP-Dronet v3 reports models down to 2.9 kB and up to 139 inferences per
second on GAP8. This supports grayscale as a credible edge path, but it does not
remove the need to test lower resolutions for FlightRL's task and camera.

Recent vision-based agile-flight work uses a privileged-state teacher,
imitation learning for the visual student, and RL fine-tuning. That sequence
matches FlightRL's intended teacher, replay, shadow, and bounded-live gates:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12203

Recent nano-UAV work also supports separating global visual perception from
local geometric safety. PULP-Dronet plus an `8x8` depth map outperformed either
input alone in the reported navigation scenarios:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11661

For language conditioning, RT-2 defines a VLA as a model trained on images,
language commands, and robot actions. FlightRL's small command-conditioned
policy therefore has the same input/output modality, but should be called
`VLN-lite` or a `small VLA policy` unless it also inherits broad vision-language
pretraining and semantic generalization:

- https://robotics-transformer2.github.io/

SINGER is a more directly relevant drone reference: language-conditioned
demonstrations generated by an expert are used to train a lightweight
closed-loop visuomotor policy. Its scale and photorealistic simulator are beyond
the first FlightRL iteration, but its teacher-to-student structure is aligned:

- https://openreview.net/forum?id=GyzYVEq4nO

## Why Not Difference-Only

Frame differences suppress static scene structure. A stationary wall corner,
door, or target can disappear when the drone stops, while exposure changes and
camera motion can produce change everywhere. Thresholded differences from a
frame camera also do not reproduce an event camera: they only observe changes
at the camera's frame rate.

Frame delta and motion masks remain useful auxiliary channels for:

- distinguishing self-motion from a static view;
- detecting moving targets;
- making short temporal trends explicit;
- testing whether a smaller recurrent network can use cheap motion cues.

They should be ablated against the current-frame baseline.

## Planned Ablations

Hold training data, policy size, seed set, and action contract fixed:

| Variant | Channels | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| current `64x48` grayscale | 1 | minimum baseline |
| current + signed delta | 2 | cheapest motion cue |
| two grayscale frames | 2 | preserve temporal appearance |
| two frames + delta | 3 | explicit and implicit motion |
| two frames + delta + mask | 4 | sparse-change experiment |

Repeat the best channel variant at `32x24`, `64x48`, `96x72`, and `128x96`.
Evaluate navigation success, collision rate, action smoothness, stale-frame
behavior, throughput, and host/GAP8 latency. Do not select a representation from
offline loss alone.

## Compact edge transport

The first hardware implementation keeps the required current grayscale frame
but changes its wire representation. GAP8 resizes QQVGA to `64x48`, quantizes
to 4 bits, and packs two pixels per byte. The receiver expands each nibble to
8-bit grayscale before applying this observation contract.

This reduced each payload from 19,764 bytes for full QQVGA raw to 1,536 bytes.
On 2026-07-25 it sustained 64.83 FPS for 3,840 frames with radio telemetry,
zero frame drops, and zero ESP32 UDP errors. Treat 4-bit precision as an
ablation: archival frames remain higher quality, and policy selection still
depends on held-out navigation and safety metrics rather than transport speed.

Ceiling tests reached 103.12 and 110.95 FPS but exposed intermittent ESP32
allocation failures. Keep 64.83 FPS as the current zero-drop contract. The
next representation experiment should fit within one CPX payload or emit a
compact GAP8 feature tensor; sending the full sensor frame is not required for
the policy receiver to reconstruct its observation.

The one-packet experiment used `48x36` gray4 input, expanded by the receiver
into the unchanged `64x48` observation contract. It sustained 111.09 FPS for
6,600 frames with zero drops and zero UDP errors. This is transport evidence,
not proof that the lower source resolution is policy-optimal. Keep source
resolution and encoding configurable and select them through controlled policy
ablations.

## Native closed-loop renderer

The first Puffer environment uses an explicit three-dimensional simulator, not
a learned world model. Native six-DoF state and firmware-style setpoint control
advance the drone. A pinhole camera attached at the AI Deck mount pose raycasts
the room and obstacle geometry at every 65 Hz control tick.

The current policy tensor contains 9,222 floats:

```text
3,072  current 64x48 gray4 appearance, normalized to [-1, 1]
3,072  signed current-minus-previous difference
3,072  thresholded motion mask
    6  body-frame target direction, distance, and yaw-error sin/cos
```

Each appearance pixel is one of the 16 gray4 levels. Scene seed, obstacle
geometry and offset, exposure target, texture, and sensor noise vary by
environment. Puffer's environment index must remain part of the random seed;
overwriting it duplicates one scene across all agents and invalidates parallel
training evidence.

This renderer is sufficient to test causal geometry and the complete
camera-to-action pipeline. It is not yet a realistic room renderer. Semantic
navigation will require varied three-dimensional objects, materials, lights,
real-room textures, motion blur, lens effects, and held-out real-frame tests.
A learned world model is not required for this stage: richer explicit 3D scenes
can remain the observation function of the native Puffer environment.

## First Puffer visual result

On 2026-07-26 the native environment reached about 169k agent-steps/s without
the network. The compact CNN/MinGRU training loop reached about 1.1k
agent-steps/s on the local CPU.

The waypoint baseline reached 100% deterministic success in clear scenes. The
first residual visual checkpoints did not pass the causal obstacle gate:
obstructed full-camera and camera-masked evaluations both produced 0% success
and 100% collision. Training-time success above the 25% clear-scene prior came
from stochastic residual exploration, not a camera-dependent deterministic
mean. These checkpoints are simulation artifacts only and are not approved for
live flight.

The next experiment must report obstructed full-camera versus masked-camera
success and reject checkpoints without a material gap. Do not infer visual
learning from PPO return or stochastic rollout success alone.

## GAP8 deployment reference

`gemenerik/gap8-obstacle-avoidance` is useful for the on-edge deployment lane:
it demonstrates paired QQVGA grayscale frames, NanoFlowNet optical flow,
NNTool/SQ8 generation, and GAP8 C kernels on the AI Deck. It can provide an
optical-flow auxiliary feature or teacher, while Puffer remains responsible for
closed-loop navigation training.

- https://github.com/gemenerik/gap8-obstacle-avoidance
- https://github.com/tudelft/nanoflownet
